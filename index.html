Read the scoring window before the protocol

Biohacking results get quoted as verdicts when they are really answers to a window. The XPRIZE Healthspan audit at HackedSelf makes the point concretely: results measured at 90 days were being scored against a 12-month frame, so an early flat line said less than the press coverage implied. The same trap sits under every supplement stack and recovery gadget. Decide the window before the first capsule, and decide what a real signal would look like inside it.

The cost half is plain arithmetic. Five items at $30 each runs $150 a month, which is $5 a day and $1,800 a year. A 90-day self-trial spends $450 and samples a quarter of the year. If the honest endpoint is twelve months, budget the whole $1,800 up front, because a stack that stops at day 90 answers a question the purchase was not sized for. Gadgets price the same way: cost per session, sessions per week, window before judgment.

Dose discipline is the other half of the audit. The sauna protocol on the site takes 4 sessions above 175F before cardiovascular claims get a hearing. Strength progress gets measured on grip, gait, and lower-body power instead of mirror impressions. Sleep fixes start with a locked wake time, not a new device. Each of those is a dose and a check, and each fails quietly when the dose is guessed.

So run the stack like any other line item: fixed window, fixed dose, fixed check, cost per day that survives the math. Interventions that clear it stay. The rest were expensive placeholders.

James Verlander, HackedSelf
